# Version 2: rebuild plan

Version 1.0 is tagged `v1.0` (`f18c97e`). Version 2 rebuilds the app one
feature at a time. Each feature sits behind a PostHog feature flag and is
switched on when it is done.

Inputs:

- the KSV guide `reference/KSV_M3.pdf`, which stays the source of truth for
  positions;
- the FIVB Official Volleyball Rules 2025-2028, which decide what is legal;
- the video "How To Run A 5-1 Volleyball Rotation (ANIMATED GUIDE)" by
  Conan Liu, M.D.: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkpmYtogPdw (15:04). It
  gives the look, the animation style and some explanations.

Version 2 replaces version 1 in place, at the same URL. A v1 feature stays
hidden until its v2 issue is done. The GitHub milestones "v2: Learn" and
"v2: Later" track the work; see "Working on v2 issues" in `CLAUDE.md`.

What version 2 changes:

1. The court is modelled on the video, with contrast fixed for a phone.
2. Our serve follows the 2025 rule, not the older rule the video uses.
3. Animated explanations show how players move between phases, including
   where the middle and the libero go.
4. There are two rule sets. The default is **Simplified KSV**, a training
   convention with one middle and the libero always on. **Official** is the
   KSV guide with MB1, MB2 and legal libero replacements.

## 1. Feature flags

### How the flags work

The app must still work from `file://`, offline and in the tests, so the
flags only override values that are built into the file.

- **Defaults:** a `FEATURES` object in `template.html` holds the default of
  every flag. A feature that is not built yet defaults to `false`.
- **Checks:** every feature checks `feature("key")` before it renders its tab,
  button or step. Hidden features leave no empty space.
- **Tab bar:** when only one tab is on, the tab bar is hidden.
- **PostHog override:** on `xszpo.github.io`, the PostHog flag values replace
  the defaults.
  - Today `analyticsLoad()` sets `advanced_disable_flags: true`. Version 2
    removes that and keeps everything else in the privacy set-up.
  - The last values are stored in `ksv51:flags` and passed to PostHog as
    `bootstrap.featureFlags`. The next visit then renders at once, without
    waiting for the network.
  - `posthog.onFeatureFlags()` applies new values only if the user has not
    touched the page yet. Otherwise they apply on the next load. Tabs that
    move under a finger cause mis-taps.
- **On or off only:** persistence is `memory`, so every page load gets a new
  anonymous id. Percentage rollouts and per-person targeting therefore do not
  work. Each flag is either off, or on at 100%.
- **Preview on one phone:**
  - `?ff=all` turns every flag on for that device.
  - `?ff=key1,-key2` sets single flags.
  - Either is stored in `ksv51:ffOverride`, and `?ff=reset` clears it.
  - `?ff=` applies at once.
  - This is how the owner tries a feature on the live site before switching
    it on for everyone.
- **Tests:** the tests open the app with `?ff=all`. A flag test checks that a
  feature that is off leaves no trace in the DOM.
- **Flag reads:** each page load reads the flags once. The free tier includes
  1M flag requests a month.
- **Version property:** `posthog.register({app_version: "2"})` runs at load,
  so every event carries `app_version: "2"`. v1 and v2 usage can then be told
  apart.

### Build order

1. The shell and rules model, then `learn-tab`.
2. `learn-animation`, for Simplified.
3. `rules-official`, with the Official exchange steps.
4. `drill-tab`, `neighbour-check` and `drill-review`.
5. `match-solo`, then `sets-tab`, `sets-quiz` and `set-call-check`.
6. `match-same-device`, then `match-online`.
7. `downloads`, `learn-guides` and `rotations-table`.
8. `after-dig`, once the coach has confirmed the spots.

Each step is one PR. It switches the flag on after merge, once the owner has
checked it with `?ff=key`. It also adds a tracking event where a v1 event
covered the feature. v1 analytics events stay the same.

Once every flag has been on for a few weeks, remove the flag checks and delete
the flags. A flag left in code for ever is dead weight.

### 2.3 Court

- **Proportions:** true 9 × 9 m, so the court is square. The video's 1.5:1
  does not fit a portrait phone.
- **SVG viewBox:** `-4 -14 108 127`. The court is 0..100 on both axes, so a
  spot `(x, y)` from `data.py` is `(100x, 100y)`. The 14 units above the net
  hold the `NET` label. The 13 units below the end line hold the server and
  the "off court" pill, where SUB and the libero wait.
- **3 m line:** at `ATTACK_LINE` 0.42, as in the guide.
- **Markers:** diameter 12 units, about 40 px on a 390 px phone. The tap
  target is an invisible 44 px circle. The closest pair in `data.py` (OH1 and
  S, R1 reception) is 49 px apart, so hit areas do not clash.
- **Minimum marker size:** 36 px. Below that, shrink the court height cap,
  not the markers.
- **Label:** Barlow Condensed 700, 5.2 units (about 17 px), role and number,
  for example `OH1`. L's label is `#3a2a00`.
- **Zone numbers:** off by default. A "Zones" toggle in the options fold
  draws them in `--court-line` at 40% opacity.
- **Hidden teammates:** in Drill and Match, "Nobody" removes them from the
  DOM. This stays the default.
- **Faded teammates** (Learn, "Only me"): 35% opacity, label kept, no pointer
  events.
- **SUB:** grey fill with a dashed edge, so it is not mistaken for a faded
  player.
- **Boundaries (T-bars):** your own overlap limits. One line per overlap
  partner, from your marker edge along or across the court to the line that
  partner sets, in the partner's route colour, with a 6.4 unit T-bar at the
  end. A limit inside your marker has no line; the caption names every
  partner ("Overlap: stay in front of L, right of OP and left of OH1.").
  Shown only in Rotation and Reception, and only when you are on court.
- **Ball:** a flat white circle, 6.4 units, a 0.5 unit `--marker-edge`
  outline and two blue seams. It flies passer → setter → hitter.
- **Off court:** a dashed pill, "off court", in `--court-line`, bottom left
  under the end line.

### 2.4 Your player

## 3. Our serve (2025 rule)

The video is out of date here. At 4:31 it says "as soon as the ball is
contacted the rules of rotation no longer apply". Its Service slides bunch the
front row in the middle, then show SWITCH.

Version 2 teaches the current rule (FIVB 2025-2028 rule 7.4, used by
Volleyball Danmark since September 2025). The rule says: "The players of the
serving team, however, are free to occupy any position at the service hit."

- **When we serve, there is no overlap rule.** Nobody has to stay in order
  with their neighbours. We still serve in the correct service order.
- **Rows still count.** The row is fixed by the rotation, not by where you
  stand. Only front-row players may block. A back-row player may attack at
  any height only if the take-off foot has not touched or crossed the 3 m
  line. After the hit they may land inside it. From the front zone, a
  back-row player may only hit a ball that is partly below the top of the
  net.
- **Before the serve, everyone except the server stands in their base
  spot.** There is no bunching and no switch. The server serves from
  anywhere behind the end line and runs to their base spot. The app draws
  only that arrow.
- **Base spots stay spread out.** A group standing together to hide the
  serve is a screen (rule 12.5).
- **The overlap boundaries (T-bars) show only in Rotation and Reception.**

Where the app explains the rule, it says why: "The serving team may stand
anywhere when the ball is served. You stay in your row for play: a back-row
player may not block."

This keeps the v1 `serve` phase and `BASE_DEF`. The video contributes only
the look of the server's run.

## 4. Animated explanations (`learn-animation`)

The video's strength is watching every player move at once. Version 2
animates four transitions for each rotation. It ships for Simplified first.
The Official exchange steps come with `rules-official`.

### 4.1 Transitions

| From → to | What moves |
|-----------|------------|
| Rotation → Our serve | Everyone to base, the server behind the end line. Into R3 and R6 (Official): the libero leaves and the front-row middle comes on first |
| Our serve → Reception | We lost the rally: a dead-ball walk to the reception spots from the guide. In R3 and R6 (Official) the libero comes on for the middle in zone 1. In the other rotations the libero is already on |
| Reception → After reception | Stages: the setter releases first, then the attackers move to approach, then the passers take cover spots. The ball flies passer → setter → hitter |
| After reception → next Rotation | Caption: "The rally goes on. We win the point and rotate." Everyone moves one zone clockwise (2 → 1 → 6 → 5 → 4 → 3 → 2). Simplified, into R3 and R6: the middle pair resets (section 5) |

- Our serve is the first serve of the rotation. Serving more points does not
  rotate.
- The app has no phase for the rally after our attack until `after-dig`. No
  "go to base" banner shows at After reception.

### 4.2 What plays and when

- **No autoplay.** The page opens on the static end state, with routes
  drawn.
- **The primary button plays the next transition.** It reads
  `Next: After reception ▸` and moves the tag, the players and the caption
  together. After the last phase it reads `Next: R2 (S6) ▸`. It follows
  `STEP_ORDER`.
- **Phase chips:** tapping the next phase plays that transition once. Any
  other jump snaps to the end state with no motion.
- **Replay** restarts the current transition.

### 4.3 Timing

| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Glide | 600 to 900 ms by distance (about 1 s per court width at 1×), ease-in-out quad |
| Same stage | All players of a stage start together. No stagger inside a stage |
| Hold between stages | 900 ms |
| Ball | 450 ms per leg, in parallel with its stage |
| Caption | Cross-fades over 150 ms at the start of each stage |
| End state | Stays, routes drawn, until the next tap |
| Reception → After reception | About 4.2 s at 1×, 8.4 s at 0.5× |

### 4.4 Controls

One row, 48 px, under the caption:

| Control | Behaviour |
|---------|-----------|
| `⟲` Replay | Restarts the current transition |
| `❚❚` / `▶` | Pause and play |
| `▸\|` Step | Runs one stage and pauses. Disabled while playing |
| `1×` / `0.5×` | Speed, stored in `ksv51:animSpeed` |
| `Next: … ▸` | Primary. Takes the remaining width (about 160 px) |

- The small buttons are 44 × 44. The speed button is 52 wide.
- Three stage dots sit under the row. The dot for the playing stage is
  filled. They are not buttons.
- The options fold holds `Show everyone` / `Only me` and `Zones`. Learn
  defaults to Everyone.
- Your player has the ring. The caption starts with your move in bold:
  "**You (OH1)** …".

### 4.5 Captions

- One sentence per stage, at most 90 characters (two lines at 15 px).
- Fixed two-line height, so the layout does not jump.
- Announced through an `aria-live="polite"` region. The tag is not
  announced twice.
- Say why, in plain words.
- Captions are per rotation. They come from new `move` notes in `data.py`,
  one per player per transition.
- Each caption is checked against the guide's arrows and `note` for that
  rotation. Not every hitter leaves the net the same way. In R4 to R6 the
  opposite goes to zone 1 as a back-row attacker.
- Examples:
  - "The setter leaves zone 1 for the setting spot as soon as the serve is
    hit."
  - "MB1 comes off the net to the 3 m line to approach."

### 4.6 Reduced motion and `?anim=0`

- With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` or `?anim=0`: no glide. `Next`
  cross-fades to the end state (150 ms, or 0 ms with `?anim=0`). Routes show
  at once.
- The caption shows all stages as a short list (up to three lines).
- Replay, Pause, Step and speed are hidden. The primary button stays.
- The Playwright tests use `?anim=0`, plus one test that plays a transition
  and checks the end positions.

### 4.7 Drill and Match

Scoring speed must not slow down, and Next never waits.

- **No multi-stage animation between questions.** Teammates are hidden by
  default, so a glide would show nothing.
- **After the answer:** your marker glides from your tap to the correct spot
  in 400 ms, with a haloed route. The correct spot is a ring outline. The
  buttons are already active, and Next cuts the glide short.
- **`Watch the move`:** an optional text button in the feedback card. It
  plays the transition into this phase for your role, in the same court, with
  the Learn controls. Next closes it.
- **Same-device and online:** no motion during a turn ("Answer saved" only).
  All motion happens on the reveal screen. Online, `Watch the move` shows
  only on the reveal.
- **Reduced motion:** the correct spot and route appear at once.

### 4.8 Implementation

- One SVG `<g>` per player. Interpolate `x` and `y` in
  `requestAnimationFrame` and set `transform` once per frame. No CSS
  transitions, so Step, Pause and speed are exact.
- Cancel the frame loop on tab change and on `visibilitychange`.
- The court has `touch-action: manipulation`. Markers ignore taps while a
  transition plays.
- Positions come from `players(ri, phase)`, so the animation cannot drift
  from the data.
- Nothing is added to the single file beyond the script.

### 4.9 Libero replacements (Official)

From the KSV `liberofor` and serve lineups:

| Rotation | Front-row middle | Libero in reception replaces | Rotation and Our serve |
|----------|------------------|------------------------------|------------------------|
| R1 (S1) | MB1 (zone 3) | MB2 (zone 6) | L stays on for MB2 |
| R2 (S6) | MB1 (zone 2) | MB2 (zone 5) | L stays on for MB2 |
| R3 (S5) | MB2 (zone 4) | MB1 (zone 1) | **L off.** L leaves and MB2 comes on in zone 4. MB1 is in zone 1 and serves |
| R4 (S4) | MB2 (zone 3) | MB1 (zone 6) | L stays on for MB1 |
| R5 (S3) | MB2 (zone 2) | MB1 (zone 5) | L stays on for MB1 |
| R6 (S2) | MB1 (zone 4) | MB2 (zone 1) | **L off.** L leaves and MB1 comes on in zone 4. MB2 is in zone 1 and serves |

- In R3 and R6, when we lose the serve, the libero comes on for the middle
  who has been serving. This is a second replacement, and the serve was the
  completed rally between the two.
- The animation shows each replacement at the sideline by the attack line:
  one player walks off, the other walks on.
- Captions are per rotation:
  - R3: "The libero leaves; MB2 comes on in zone 4. MB1 is in zone 1 and
    serves."
  - R6: "The libero leaves; MB1 comes on in zone 4. MB2 is in zone 1 and
    serves."
- The caption states the rule: the libero may replace only a back-row
  player, and may not serve, block, or attack a ball above the net. There
  must be a completed rally between two replacements.
- `describe()` adds: after the libero sets with fingers from the front zone,
  nobody may attack that ball above the net.
- `ROWS.back` has L in zone 1 in R3 and R6, which is the reception picture.
  At the Rotation step the real lineup has the serving middle there and L
  off. Which one the Rotation step shows is question 1.

The video lets the libero serve in R3. That is a US rule. Its R3 slide also
has the libero step out and straight back in, with no rally between, which
is illegal under rule 19.3.2.1. Version 2 shows neither.

## 5. Rule sets

The `rulesMode` switch has two values: `simple` (default) and `official`.
v1's Drill mode (the libero serves in R3 and R6) is dropped until the coach
answers question 2. A stored `ksv51:rulesMode` value of `drill` maps to
`simple`. The meaning changes: in v1 Drill the libero serves, in Simplified
SUB serves.

### Simplified KSV (default)

Simplified KSV is a training convention, not a match lineup. Use it when only
one middle is there. In a match both middles play and the libero cannot
serve. The app says this in the Rules switch.

- **One middle, `MB`, always in the front row.** MB stands where the
  front-row middle stands in the guide and makes that middle's moves: MB1's
  in R6, R1 and R2; MB2's in R3, R4 and R5.
- **The libero always plays the back-row middle's spot.** The two middle
  slots are opposite: one in front, one behind.
- **The middle pair resets into R3 and into R6.** MB walks along the net
  from zone 2 to zone 4, and L walks across the back from zone 5 to zone 1.
  The other four players rotate as normal. Captions say "the middle pair
  resets".
- **In R3 and R6 both middles would be on court at the serve** (one in
  front, one in zone 1). The libero holds the zone-1 slot and cannot serve.
  A substitute (`SUB`, grey, dashed edge) serves from zone 1, then defends
  zone 6. The outside hitter keeps zone 5.
- **SUB keeps serving while we win points.** When we lose the serve, L comes
  on for SUB and takes SUB's zone, so Reception uses the guide's spot.
- **Role picker:** `MB OH1 OH2 OP S L`. MB1 and MB2 appear only in Official.
  SUB is not a pickable role.

**Data.** Simplified is derived from the official data, not typed twice.
`lineup(ri, mode)` renames the official front-row middle to MB and the middle
the libero replaces to SUB (on court only at a serve in R3 and R6); no spot
moves. `middle_pair(ri)` returns the zones of MB and L, so the reset into R3
and R6 falls out of the data; it is not a special case. `build.py` emits both
rule sets and the app swaps `ROWS` on a switch. `tests/audit.py` checks both
modes and the walk-through tables below.

### Official (`rules-official`)

- A restore of v1's `official` mode: the KSV guide positions with MB1 and
  MB2.
- The libero replaces the back-row middle in reception, and is off court
  while a middle serves in R3 and R6 (section 4.9).

## 7. Hazards

- **Database rules:** online play validates `role` against a fixed list in
  `infra/database.rules.json`. The file accepts `MB` and `meta.rulesMode`
  `simple` (#19), but the live rules change only with a `terraform apply` by
  the owner, which `match-online` needs before its flag goes on.
- **Stored data:** bests and stats are keyed by role and rules mode. Version 2
  moves the best key prefix to `v5|`, and Simplified MB stats start empty.
  Stored `drill` reads as `simple`.
- **Cheat sheets:** regenerate them on white with the new role fills. Keep
  DejaVu Sans rendering, as in v1.
- **Contrast:** `tests/theme_test.py` gains a check that reads the court and
  role tokens and asserts the ratios in section 2.2. The next colour tweak
  cannot then break them silently.

## 8. Open questions for the coach

1. **Rotation step in R3 and R6:** show the real lineup (the serving middle
   in zone 1, L off), or L in zone 1 as in the guide's "Starting rotation"
   boxes? The same applies to SUB in Simplified.
2. **One middle and nobody else:** who serves in R3 and R6, a bench player
   (SUB) or the libero? If the libero serves in training, v1's Drill rule
   comes back as a named training option.
3. **Where the server defends in R3 and R6:** the serving middle (Official)
   or SUB (Simplified) in zone 6 with the OH in zone 5, as `data.py` has it
   now? Or zone 5 with the OH in zone 6?
4. **The Simplified reset:** does MB really walk along the net from zone 2 to
   zone 4 into R3 and R6? Or does MB rotate to the back row and another
   player go up front?
5. **Libero out of zone 1 (Official R3/R6):** may the libero replace the
   serving middle as soon as we lose the serve, or does the middle stay in
   for the first ball?
6. **Base spots:** can the front-row base spots at Our serve stay at mid-zone
   (y 0.21), or should they be at the net?
7. **Set calls:** what do "Po" and "4" mean, and which calls is each
   front-row player asked?
8. **Cheat sheets:** should the printed sheets and the all-rotations table
   show Official or Simplified?
